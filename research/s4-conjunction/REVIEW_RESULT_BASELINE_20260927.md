Is the baseline real evidence that the hierarchical multi-modal / symptom-alphabet direction is worth pursuing? Modestly, yes — as a *negative-space* result. What survives after stripping the by-construction parts: (1) the modality that reaches a fault depends on the fault class, not on the target: soft faults (latency, timeout, exception) leave no log template anywhere in G-old and are visible only as non-target trace absence; hard faults are announced by the SUT's own service-discovery and connection-handling logs in non-target services; on Hotel the same fault class on two targets gives 72/72 vs 0/72 logs. The strongest non-trivial finding is the G-old per-class modality split (WARN+ logs 6 hard-fault families at 10/10, FA 0/30; spans-only for the 4 soft-fault families at 9/10, FA 0/30; and the exact `f_all` showing four image-operation absence sensors cover 31/110 episodes), with the Hotel log asymmetry as its two-target counterpart.

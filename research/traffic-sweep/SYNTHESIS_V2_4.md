## What follows for the method

- **By construction, not a finding:** the operating point is family-level (one K per family, decision 53), a family's
  alarms are the union over every sensor and rule in its scored set (unscreened, H1), and B1 makes P4 `—` when any
  family is off. So one uncalibratable rule removes a family, and one family removes P4 at that (class, rate).
- **Observed on three instances:** what removes a family is a handful of rules per system, and most of them do not
  scale with K: `absent` and `appear` have no K, two-state and quantised series defeat a median + K·MAD rule, and the
  `absent` gap is armed only once a template has 20 pooled records. Adding quiet cells did not change the systems; it
  armed rules that the 4 sweep cells left unarmed. More quiet time also lowers the bound for a given count (0 alarms:
  5.45/h at 0.55 h, 4.28/h at 0.70 h).
- **Literature note** (`research/protocol/LIT_DETECTION_RULES.md`, Q3 recommendation, lines 131-135): declare a FAR per
  hour, calibrate **each sensor's** combined rule to it, and report the placement-level FAR beside every placement
  score. Decision 53 chose the family as the unit instead. A bound on the placement inside the objective (Σ f_i ≤ ρ) is
  D29's device (one paper, water networks); the note's row 7 keeps it a user decision, and its Q6 advises against D29's
  assumption that sensors are independent.
- **Item 4 of the same note** (line 268): a setting changed after OTel's fault cells were read ends OTel's role as a
  test. My inference: any change now makes this round exploratory on all three systems, and campaign B (3 more blocks
  on TeaStore and OTel, decision 49) would be the first data read under it.

## Decisions for the user (options listed, none chosen)

1. **Calibration unit.** Keep one K per family (decision 53); or calibrate per sensor (the note's recommendation), a
   sensor/rule that cannot meet its budget on quiet data being not placeable and listed; or per rule.
2. **Placement-level FAR.** Report it beside each placement (note), or also bound it inside the objective, Σ f_i ≤ ρ
   (D29; assumes independent sensors).
3. **Rules without K** (`absent`, `appear`): their false-alarm rate is set by the gap estimate and the 20-record
   minimum. Options: a gap multiplier or minimum gap fitted to the budget; a period-aware gap; declaring
   deployment-locked one-shot templates (startup, maintenance, GoAway) not `absent`-eligible.
4. **Series a median + K·MAD rule cannot calibrate:** quantile (empirical) thresholds for two-state and bimodal series
   (decision 52 already names the quantile threshold as a sensitivity row, W2); a quantum-aware floor for quantised
   counters (MAD just above 0 escapes decision 52).
5. **The ledger's own calibration:** OTel E at r10 fails; no sensor-side choice changes that.
6. **Target and criterion.** 15/h (decision 33) or ≤ 6/h (the quiet runs' sizing, decision 48); upper bound (as now) or
   point estimate; the certifiable count at the current quiet time is 3 (TeaStore, OTel) or 4 (Hotel) alarms per rate.
   The K grid stops at 100 (decision 55).
7. **P4 when a family is off** (B1): keep `—`, or compare over the calibrated families and name the excluded ones.
